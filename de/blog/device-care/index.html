Charging, cleaning and backups: looking after a communication device

A few simple habits keep a device working, protect custom words and photos, and avoid the panic of a flat battery at the wrong moment.

Besprich Entscheidungen für dein Kind mit der Kinderärztin oder dem Kinderarzt, der Logopädin oder dem Logopäden oder einer anderen qualifizierten Fachperson.

A communication device is used all day, handled by children, carried in bags and taken to places where phones get dropped. It needs looking after, not because it is fragile, but because it is important.

Good device care is mostly boring: charge it, clean it, back it up, know what to do when it fails. Those habits prevent a lot of silent, frustrating days.

Make charging part of the routine

  • Charge overnight, every night.
  • Keep a charger in the bag and one at school or work.
  • Use a power bank for long days out.
  • Check the cable for fraying; replace damaged cables.
  • Avoid charging in a hot car or direct sun.

If the battery is draining quickly, check screen brightness, background apps and battery health. A device that dies every afternoon is an access problem, not a behaviour problem.

Clean it safely

Follow the manufacturer’s instructions first. In general:

  • wipe the screen with a soft, slightly damp microfibre cloth;
  • use a case that can be wiped;
  • avoid spraying liquid directly onto the device;
  • keep drinks, paint, sand and glue away from the screen;
  • clean sticky fingers before using the device when possible.

For a device used at meals or in personal care, a wipe-clean case and a paper backup are practical. Do not use harsh chemicals unless the manufacturer says they are safe.

Back up custom work

Custom photos, recorded voices, vocabulary and settings can take hours to recreate. Back them up:

  • use the app’s export or backup feature if it has one;
  • keep a copy of custom photos in a separate folder;
  • photograph the board layout;
  • note any settings that were hard to find;
  • test a restore before you need it.

Store the backup somewhere you can find it, not only on the device that might be lost.

Update carefully

Updates can fix bugs and improve voices, but they can also change behaviour. Before updating:

  • check whether the person relies on the device today;
  • back up first;
  • update at a quiet time, not five minutes before school;
  • test the voice, the board and any custom audio afterwards.

If an update causes a problem, contact the app’s support rather than reinstalling immediately. A reinstall may erase local data.

Protect the device in transit

  • Use a case with a screen cover.
  • Attach a strap or handle if the person tends to drop it.
  • Keep it in a dedicated pocket in the bag, not loose with keys.
  • Use a mount in the car if the person needs it while travelling.
  • Bring a paper backup for places where the device could be damaged.

If the device is lost or stolen

  • Change any account passwords connected to the device.
  • Use a find-my or device-location feature if available.
  • Report the loss to the school, workplace or transport provider.
  • Tell the person’s communication partners so they can support with a backup.
  • Review what personal information was stored and what needs to be changed.

A monthly five-minute check

Once a month:

  1. Charge fully and check the battery.
  2. Clean the screen and case.
  3. Open the backup and confirm it is current.
  4. Test the voice at the volume used in a noisy room.
  5. Check that the paper backup matches the app.

Five minutes of maintenance can save a day of frustration. The device is a voice; looking after it is part of looking after the person’s communication.
