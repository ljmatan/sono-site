Paper first: the no-battery board that goes everywhere

A printed board is instant, robust and easy to personalise. How to make a paper copy that matches the app and keeps communication available when technology cannot.

Questo articolo non è ancora stato tradotto nella tua lingua, quindi è mostrato in inglese. Aiutaci a tradurlo

Non è un consiglio medico. Questo articolo condivide informazioni generali ed esperienze quotidiane. Non è un consiglio medico, terapeutico o diagnostico. Per le decisioni che riguardano tuo figlio o tua figlia, parlane con il medico, il logopedista o un altro professionista qualificato.

A communication app is convenient, but it is not always practical. The tablet is charging, the pool is wet, the battery is flat, or a stranger is holding the queue. A paper board is not a lesser version of the app. It is the same voice in a format that never needs a battery.

Many AAC teams recommend a low-tech backup alongside a device. The Communication Bill of Rights asks for a working communication system at all times, and paper is one of the simplest ways to honour that. The Keep it within reach post looks at the wider question of access.

Why paper still matters

  • It starts instantly. No unlocking, no waiting for an app, no loading.
  • It survives places devices do not. Baths, beaches, muddy walks, meals, swimming lessons.
  • It is easy to share. A stranger can understand a printed board more quickly than a tablet they have never seen.
  • It cannot be silenced by a flat battery. A charger is not always nearby.
  • It can be personalised cheaply. Print, draw, cut and stick.

Paper is not a step backwards. It is another route to the same words, and having more than one route makes communication more reliable.

Make one from the app

The easiest paper board is a copy of the screen the person already knows.

  1. Open the board you use most.
  2. Take a screenshot or photograph the screen.
  3. Print it at a readable size, or ask a print shop to laminate it.
  4. Add the same words in the same positions, so switching between paper and app does not mean learning a new layout.
  5. Print a second copy for the school bag or the car.

If the board has many pages, start with the main page and the two or three categories used most often. A small, well-used board beats a thick folder nobody opens.

Three formats worth having

  • A placemat board. Laminate the main page for the table. It wipes clean and stays in the middle of the meal.
  • A keyring card. Shrink the core words to credit-card size, punch a hole and attach it to a bag or belt loop. Useful on walks and in shops.
  • A wallet card. A folded card with HELP, STOP, YES, NO, PAIN and an emergency contact. Useful for adults and teenagers who do not want to carry a full folder.

Keep the words consistent with the app. If STOP is red in the app, keep it red on paper. If a word moves in the app, update the paper copy at the same time.

What to put on it

A useful paper board usually has:

  • core words: I, YOU, WANT, MORE, STOP, GO, HELP, ALL DONE, LIKE, NOT;
  • a way to say yes and no;
  • the person’s name and the names of important people;
  • a few personal words: a favourite food, activity, place or person;
  • a way to ask for a break or say something is wrong;
  • an emergency contact, if appropriate.

You do not need every symbol. A board with ten well-chosen words that are used every day is more useful than a crowded page nobody can scan.

Keep it where the moment happens

A paper board only works if it is within reach. Put one in the kitchen, one in the car, one in the school bag and one in the bathroom. If the person uses a wheelchair, attach a small board where they can reach it.

Do not save the paper board for emergencies only. Use it in ordinary moments too, so it stays familiar and the person does not associate it with something going wrong.

Look after it

Paper gets wet, crumpled and lost. Laminate the main copies. Take a photo of each board so you can print a replacement. Keep a spare in a drawer.

If a board is damaged, replace it rather than patching it with tape that covers words. A tatty board is still a voice, but a clear one is easier to use.

A small weekend project

This weekend, print one page from the app. Put it on the table at breakfast. Model two words from it. Notice whether the paper changes how often the person communicates, or whether it simply makes a hard moment easier. Either answer is useful.
