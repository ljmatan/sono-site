Mealtimes: the easiest place to start with AAC

Food brings choices, repetition and a natural reason to communicate. How to use breakfast, snacks and dinner without turning the table into a lesson.

Tätä artikkelia ei ole vielä käännetty kielellesi, joten se näytetään englanniksi. Auta meitä kääntämään se

Ei lääketieteellinen neuvo. Tämä artikkeli jakaa yleistä tietoa ja arjen kokemuksia. Se ei ole lääketieteellinen, terapeuttinen eikä diagnostinen neuvo. Keskustele lastasi koskevista päätöksistä hänen lääkärinsä, puheterapeuttinsa tai muun pätevän ammattilaisen kanssa.

Mealtimes happen every day, several times a day. The same words come back: more, all done, want, drink, help. That repetition makes meals one of the easiest places to start with a symbol board, and one of the easiest places to model without setting aside extra time.

It is also a place where pressure can creep in. Food is emotional. The aim is not a perfect communication session; it is a slightly easier meal.

Start with a real choice

A choice is only useful if both options are possible. Hold up two foods the person actually eats, or show two symbols:

“JUICE or WATER?”

“APPLE or BANANA?”

Then wait. Accept a tap, a point, a look or a reach. If they choose the same thing every day, that is still a choice.

Avoid offering a choice when only one answer is acceptable. If the person must take medicine, say, “Medicine now. Then you can choose a drink.” Honesty builds trust.

Keep the key words close

At the table, a few words do most of the work:

  • MORE
  • ALL DONE
  • WANT
  • HELP
  • DRINK
  • EAT
  • STOP

If the board is on a shelf across the room, it will not be used. Put it on the table, or use a paper placemat version. The person should be able to reach it without asking.

Model during the meal

You do not need to stop eating and run a lesson. Tap a word as you say it:

“MORE pasta?”

“Mmm, YUMMY.”

“I’m ALL DONE.”

Model the words you use yourself, not only the ones you want the person to say. Meals are full of comments, not just requests.

Do not use food as a test

It is tempting to withhold a favourite food until the person taps the right symbol. That turns hunger into a quiz and can make the board feel like a barrier rather than a help.

If the person is hungry and reaches for the food, give them some and model the word. Communication should make the meal easier, not harder.

Manage the messy parts

Mealtimes are messy. A device can get splashed, sticky or dropped. A few practical habits help:

  • Use a wipe-clean case or a paper board at the table.
  • Keep drinks away from the device.
  • Print a small board for the highchair or the table.
  • If the person throws the device, treat it as a safety issue and use the paper backup while you work out a plan.

None of this means the device should be removed for the whole meal. A backup is not a replacement.

End the meal clearly

“ALL DONE” is a powerful phrase. When the person shows they have finished, model it, say it, and let them be finished. If they say ALL DONE and you keep offering food, the word stops working.

Meals are also a good place for small social words: THANK YOU, PLEASE, YUMMY, YUCK. Model them naturally. A shared joke at the table is communication too.

A small experiment

For one week, put the board on the table at every meal. Choose two words to model. Do not test. At the end of the week, notice which words appeared without prompting, and which moments were easiest. Build the next week around those.
