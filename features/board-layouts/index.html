Make it their own

Three board layouts: Simple, Advanced and Menu

Choose how much the screen shows, from one tap for one word to full sentence building, and try each layout before you decide.

Where to find it Caretaker Settings > Board Display > Board Layout

Get it on Google Play How to use it

Sono is a free app for augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) on Android: the person taps pictures, and the app speaks for them.

People communicate in different ways, and the same person may need a different screen on a tired day. Sono offers three layouts of the same vocabulary. Switching between them never loses symbols, photos or history.

What you can do

  • Simple: category tabs and symbols only, with no sentence strip. One tap says one word. The quietest screen, and the easiest for a first start, for taking turns with single words and for modelling.
  • Advanced: the full board with category tabs, subcategory chips and the sentence strip, for people who join words into sentences.
  • Menu: one category at a time with big tiles, plus the sentence strip. For people who tire with busy screens or who tap imprecisely.
  • Tapping a layout switches the board to it straight away and shows it in a live preview, and Try it full-screen lets you test it properly.

How to use it

  1. Open Caretaker Settings > Board Display.
  2. Under Board Layout, tap Simple, Advanced or Menu. The board switches straight away, and the preview shows how it looks.
  3. Tap Try it full-screen to test the layout, then close the preview.
  4. Go back to the board and watch how the person gets on with it.

Good to know

  • A new install starts in Menu, and the first-run setup lets you choose a different layout straight away.
  • Tap Behavior, on the same screen, decides whether a double tap (the default) or a single tap adds a word to the sentence.
  • Not sure which to choose? The usage guide describes who each layout suits, and a speech and language therapist can help you decide.

Questions and answers

Which layout is best for someone who is just starting?

The Simple layout is made for a first start: one tap says one word, and there is no sentence strip to manage. Every person is different, so try each layout, and ask a speech and language therapist if you can.

Do I lose anything when I switch layouts?

No. All three layouts use the same vocabulary, photos and history, so you can switch at any time.
