When the device isn't being used: a troubleshooting checklist

A communication app that sits unused is not a failed experiment. Work through access, motivation, vocabulary and partner habits before changing the whole system.

Cet article n'est pas encore traduit dans votre langue ; il est donc affiché en anglais. Aidez-nous à le traduire

Pas un avis médical. Cet article partage des informations générales et des expériences du quotidien. Il ne constitue pas un avis médical, thérapeutique ou diagnostique. Pour toute décision concernant votre enfant, parlez-en à son médecin, à son orthophoniste ou à un autre professionnel qualifié.

You bought the app, set up the board, modelled for a week, and nothing happened. Or the person used it for a while and then stopped. It is easy to conclude that AAC does not work for them, or that you did something wrong.

Usually, the answer is smaller than that. One part of the system is not working: access, motivation, vocabulary, timing or the behaviour of communication partners. A checklist helps you find the part before you rebuild everything.

1. Is the device actually available?

  • Is it charged?
  • Is it within reach, not on a shelf or in a bag?
  • Is it unlocked and on the right board?
  • Does the person know where it is?
  • Is a paper backup available?

A surprising number of “won’t use it” situations are “can’t reach it” situations.

2. Is the vocabulary relevant?

  • Are the person’s favourite things on the board?
  • Are there words for people, places and routines that matter?
  • Is there a way to say no, stop, help and finished?
  • Are the words in predictable places?
  • Is the board too crowded or too sparse?

If the board is full of generic nouns and none of the person’s real life, it will not be motivating.

3. Is anyone modelling?

  • Do adults use the board themselves while they talk?
  • Is modelling happening in real moments, not just scheduled sessions?
  • Are other communication partners involved, or only one person?
  • Is the modelling silent and pressure-free?

A board that is only used to ask the person to perform is a test, not a communication system.

4. Is there enough wait time?

  • Do you pause after modelling?
  • Do you count to ten before repeating or rephrasing?
  • Do you fill the silence with questions?
  • Do you accept a look, gesture or sound as a response?

Many people need more time than the conversation normally allows. The ten-second pause post explains why.

5. Is the timing right?

  • Is the person tired, hungry or in pain?
  • Is the environment too noisy or busy?
  • Is the activity motivating?
  • Is the device competing with a screen or a toy?
  • Is the person being asked to communicate at the hardest moment of the day?

Try a calm, preferred moment instead of a demand-heavy one.

6. Is the access right?

  • Are the buttons big enough?
  • Can the person see the symbols clearly?
  • Is the screen angle comfortable?
  • Is pointing physically difficult?
  • Would a different layout, switch or keyboard help?

An occupational therapist can help if access is the barrier.

7. Is the person discouraged?

  • Has the device been taken away as a punishment?
  • Has communication been treated as a test?
  • Has the person been corrected repeatedly?
  • Have peers or relatives mocked the device?
  • Does the person believe their communication will be listened to?

Trust takes time to rebuild. Start with low-pressure, high-interest moments and respond to every attempt.

8. Is the system itself working?

  • Is the app up to date?
  • Is the voice audible?
  • Are custom photos or recordings intact?
  • Is the device slow or freezing?
  • Is the battery holding charge?

Technical problems are demoralising. Fix them before assuming a motivational problem.

Change one thing

Pick the most likely barrier and change one thing for two weeks. Keep a few notes: what you tried, what happened, what the person did. Do not change the board, the voice, the schedule and the partner all at once.

If nothing changes after a fair trial, ask a speech and language therapist for a review. That is not a failure. It is good practice.

A final note

AAC is a long game. A person may use a device intensely for a month, ignore it for a week, then come back. Progress is rarely a straight line. The checklist is there to help you find the next small step, not to grade anyone.
