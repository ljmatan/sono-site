Usage guide

Working together

Communication grows when everyone around the person pulls in the same direction: at home, at school and in therapy.

On this page

  1. Everyone around the person
  2. Through the day
  3. Sources and further reading

Everyone around the person # — Copy link to this section

Reviews of partner training show that teaching the people around a person who communicates with symbols how to model, wait and respond is one of the most effective things that can be done, more than training the person alone. Who's involved:

  • Family: model during the routines that already happen, meals, dressing, play, bedtime, so Sono becomes part of ordinary talk.
  • School and kindergarten: ask which communication aids they use and share the words you're working on, so both places look similar to the person.
  • Carers and assistants: keep the device within reach all day, especially during transitions and free time.
  • The therapist: ask them to show you modelling and to help pick the next words.

A quick weekly chat about which words were used, what to add and what's working keeps everyone on the same page. Backup & Restore can carry the same custom symbols to a second device.

Through the day # — Copy link to this section

Routines repeat, and the same words come up at the same moments, which makes them the natural place to model.

  • Morning: the Greetings category, EAT, DRINK and breakfast choices.
  • Mealtimes: EAT, DRINK, MORE, ALL DONE, WANT and the person's own foods. Offer two things and model both: "JUICE or WATER?" Then wait.
  • Play: GO, STOP, AGAIN, MORE, FUNNY, LOOK, TICKLE, SWING, BUBBLES. Games with a pause work best.
  • Out and about: OUTSIDE, PARK, SCHOOL, CAR, and the people you meet.
  • Feelings and bedtime: HAPPY, SAD, TIRED, HURT, and model your own feelings too. BOOK, SLEEP, goodnight.

Pick one routine to start with, not all of them, and keep the device there every day.
