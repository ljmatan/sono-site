Travel and holidays: AAC on flights, trains and unfamiliar places

A communication system can travel, but it needs a little planning. What to pack, what to prepare and how to keep a voice available far from home.

Este artículo aún no está traducido a tu idioma, por eso se muestra en inglés. Ayúdanos a traducirlo

No es consejo médico. Este artículo comparte información general y experiencias del día a día. No es un consejo médico, terapéutico ni diagnóstico. Para las decisiones sobre tu hijo o hija, habla con su médico, su logopeda u otro profesional cualificado.

Travel changes everything: the food, the routine, the language, the people and the amount of waiting. A communication system that works at home may need adjustments on the road. The aim is not to replicate home exactly. It is to keep the person’s voice available in unfamiliar places.

Pack for communication first

Before you pack clothes, check the communication kit:

  • the device, charged;
  • a charger and a power bank;
  • a cable that fits, plus a spare if possible;
  • a protective case;
  • a paper copy of the main board;
  • a small card explaining how the person communicates;
  • any offline voices or lessons already downloaded.

Put the charger in your hand luggage, not the suitcase that goes in the hold. A flat device on a long journey is hard to fix.

Prepare the journey

Show the person what will happen before you leave. Use photos or symbols for the airport, the train, the hotel or the house you are visiting.

A simple visual sequence helps:

taxi → airport → plane → hotel → beach

Model the words: GO, WAIT, PLANE, HOTEL, SLEEP, HOME. If the person uses a visual schedule at school, bring a travel version.

At security and on the plane

A communication device is part of how the person communicates, not a toy. If a security officer asks about it, a short explanation usually helps:

“This is a communication device. It speaks for my son/daughter. It needs to stay with them.”

Keep the device with the person if possible. If it must go through a scanner, explain what is happening and give it back as soon as you can. A paper board can keep communication going during the gap.

On the plane, put the device where the person can reach it, not in the overhead locker. Model words for DRINK, TOILET, MORE, ALL DONE, HELP and WAIT. Noise and pressure changes can make the journey uncomfortable; the person may need different words than usual.

New places, new words

A holiday often brings words that are not on the board: the name of a relative, a new food, a beach, a boat, a hotel. Add a few before you go, or use a temporary category. Do not rebuild the whole board; keep core words stable.

If the family speaks a different language at the destination, decide which language the board should use. The AAC in multilingual families post has ideas for keeping the home language close.

Keep routines where you can

Sleep, meals and medication can all shift on holiday. Try to keep one or two routines stable: a bedtime story, a morning drink, a favourite song. Predictability supports communication, especially when everything else is new.

Have a plan for losing the device

Devices get left on trains, dropped at the beach or stolen. Before you go:

  • note the device’s serial number or identifying details;
  • enable a passcode and any find-my feature;
  • back up custom symbols and photos;
  • keep a paper backup in a different bag from the device;
  • know how to contact the airline or hotel lost property.

No plan can prevent every loss, but a backup means the person is not voiceless while you sort it out.

Respect the person’s limits

Travel is tiring. A person who normally uses the device may not have the energy for long conversations at the end of a day. Accept short answers, offer quiet time and do not treat a quiet day as a regression.

A small checklist

  • Device charged, plus power bank and cable.
  • Paper board in a separate bag.
  • Offline voice downloaded.
  • A few trip-specific words added.
  • A short explanation card.
  • A quiet activity for waiting.

Travel is not a test of the communication system. It is a chance to use it in a new place, with the same voice and the same people listening.
