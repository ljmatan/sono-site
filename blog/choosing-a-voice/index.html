Finding a voice that fits: accent, age and personality

The voice a communication app uses is part of someone's identity. How to choose, test and personalise a voice without defaulting to the wrong one.

Not medical advice. This article shares general information and everyday experience. It is not medical, therapeutic or diagnostic advice. For decisions about your child, talk to their doctor, speech and language therapist or another qualified professional.

A communication device does not only say words. It says them in a voice. That voice may be the first thing a stranger hears, the sound a child associates with home, or the way an adult recognises themselves in their own speech. It deserves more thought than a default setting.

Most apps offer several voices, and some allow recorded audio for individual words. The choices are practical as well as personal: speed, clarity, accent, volume and how a voice sounds in a noisy room all matter.

The default is not neutral

Many devices ship with a voice chosen by the manufacturer, often a synthetic adult voice in a widely spoken accent. For some people that is fine. For others it can feel like wearing someone else’s clothes.

Think about:

  • Age. A child may prefer a voice that sounds like a child; a teenager or adult may find a child voice embarrassing.
  • Accent and language. A voice that matches the household, region or community can feel more like the person.
  • Gender. Some people care strongly; others do not. Ask rather than assume.
  • Speed. A slower voice can be easier to understand; a faster one can keep up with conversation.
  • Volume and clarity. Test in a noisy room, not only in a quiet one.

There is no correct combination. The right voice is the one the person is comfortable using in public.

Listen together

If the person can take part, choose together. Play a few voices and ask:

  • Which one sounds most like you?
  • Which one would you be happy to use at school or work?
  • Which one is easiest to understand?
  • Which one feels too babyish, too formal or too strange?

Offer a real choice and accept the answer, even if it is not the voice you would have picked. If the person cannot answer directly, watch their reactions and try a voice for a week before deciding.

Record the words that matter

A synthetic voice is consistent and can say anything. A recorded voice can carry warmth, family jokes, a grandparent’s accent or the exact pronunciation of a name. Many apps let you record single words or phrases.

Use recordings for:

  • family names and nicknames;
  • places that matter;
  • a private joke or a comfort phrase;
  • words in a home language that the synthetic voice pronounces oddly.

Do not record everything. A mix of a clear synthetic voice and a few personal recordings often works better than a patchwork of clips.

Test in real situations

A voice that sounds good in settings may behave differently in the kitchen, the playground or a busy shop. Try it:

  • at the dinner table;
  • in the car;
  • outdoors;
  • with the person’s usual communication partners;
  • through the device’s speaker at the volume they will use.

If people keep asking the person to repeat, the voice or volume may need adjusting. That is an access issue, not a failure of the person.

Age and identity over time

A voice chosen at seven may not fit at fifteen. Revisit the choice when the person changes school, starts work, or tells you it feels wrong. The same applies to symbol style and vocabulary.

For adults, the voice can be a link to their pre-injury speech or a deliberate new sound. There is no rule that says a communication aid must preserve a former voice, and no rule that says it must abandon one. The person decides.

A note on “sounding natural”

Synthetic voices are not a test of authenticity. A person using a device is communicating, not performing. If a listener finds the voice unusual, that is a listener problem to solve with patience, not a reason for the person to hide the device.

Choose a voice that lets the person be understood and feel like themselves. Everything else is secondary.

In Sono

Sono lets you choose from available system and offline voices and record custom audio for individual symbols. The Caretaker settings guide explains where these options live. Try one change at a time, and keep a paper backup with the same words.
